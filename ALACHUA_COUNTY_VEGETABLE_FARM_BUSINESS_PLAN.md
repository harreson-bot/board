The farm will serve three revenue streams: direct-to-consumer (farmers market), CSA (Community Supported Agriculture), and wholesale (local restaurants and grocers).

- Startup Investment: $45,000 - $65,000
- First Year Revenue Projection: $35,000 - $50,000

- Farm cannot exceed 30% of Alachua County average (116 acres), so max 34.8 acres qualifies for beginning farmer programs
- This 20-30 acre plan qualifies for FSA Beginning Farmer Loans and NIFA training programs

## SECTION 3: CROP SELECTION & PRODUCTION PLAN

### 3.1 Crop Mix (20 Acres)

#### Tomatoes (6 acres)
- **Varieties:** Cherry, heirloom, determinate (season-dependent)
- **Season:** March-June, September-November (avoid summer heat)
- **Yield:** 15-25 tons/acre
- **Revenue/Acre:** $3,000-6,000/acre
- **Labor:** High (staking, pruning, daily harvest)
- **Markets:** Farmers market (best), wholesale (some waste)
- **Challenges:** Disease (early/late blight), heat tolerance, water management

#### Lettuce & Greens (4 acres)
- **Varieties:** Romaine, butterhead, mesclun, spinach, arugula
- **Season:** October-April (cool season), some summer with shade
- **Yield:** 10,000-15,000 lbs/acre
- **Revenue/Acre:** $2,500-4,500/acre
- **Labor:** Medium (harvest, washing, packaging)
- **Markets:** Farmers market (excellent), CSA (core product), wholesale (high demand)
- **Challenges:** Cooling post-harvest, packaging, shelf life (5-7 days)

#### Strawberries (4 acres)
- **Varieties:** Day-neutral (year-round), June-bearing (March-May)
- **Season:** February-June primary, year-round possible
- **Yield:** 6,000-12,000 lbs/acre
- **Revenue/Acre:** $4,000-8,000/acre
- **Labor:** Very high (delicate harvest, short shelf life)
- **Markets:** Farmers market (premium price), pick-your-own (agritourism), local sales
- **Challenges:** Disease management, cooling, short shelf life (3-4 days)

#### Onions (3 acres)
- **Varieties:** Sweet, red, yellow (storage varieties)
- **Season:** October-April (plant), May-July (harvest)
- **Yield:** 12,000-18,000 lbs/acre
- **Revenue/Acre:** $1,500-3,000/acre
- **Labor:** Low-medium (mechanizable)
- **Markets:** Farmers market, CSA, wholesale, storage/year-round sales
- **Challenges:** Long season, disease (fungal), storage requirements

#### Other Crops (3 acres - rotation, experimental)
- Green beans, squash, peppers, cucumbers, herbs
- Seasonal variety for market competitiveness
- Wholesale trial crops

## SECTION 9: GAP CERTIFICATION (Good Agricultural Practices)

### 9.1 Do You NEED GAP Certification?

**Short Answer:** Depends on your market.

**YES, you need it if selling to:**
- Major grocery chains (Publix, Whole Foods, etc.)
- Large wholesale accounts
- Food service with strict requirements
- Food safety compliance requirements

**NO, you don't if selling only to:**
- Farmers market (direct-to-consumer)
- CSA (direct-to-consumer)
- Local restaurants (vary, but often not required)
- Some small retailers (ask first)

### 9.2 What is GAP Certification?

**GAP = Good Agricultural Practices**
- **Type:** Voluntary audit, not a license
- **Purpose:** Verify food safety practices (water, sanitation, worker hygiene, pest management, record-keeping)
- **Cost:** $500-2,000+ per audit (one-time or annual)
- **Duration:** 30 days to 90 days from request to completion
- **Validity:** 3 years

### 9.3 GAP Certification Process

**Step 1: Prepare Your Farm**
- Water testing (source safety)
- Sanitation plan (harvest, packing, storage areas)
- Worker health/hygiene policies
- Pest management records
- Record-keeping system for traceability
- Equipment cleaning procedures

**Step 2: Complete SC-430 Vendor Form (USDA)**
- Establishes billing account with USDA AMS (Agricultural Marketing Service)
- One-time setup
- Submit: https://www.ams.usda.gov/resources/sc430

**Step 3: Submit Audit Request (Form SC-237A & SC-651)**
- Request audit service from USDA AMS
- Specify audit type (e.g., "USDA Harmonized GAP")
- Select nearest audit office (Florida office: multiple locations)

**Step 4: USDA Auditor Visit**
- Announced audit: ~40-80 hours of review
- Unannounced verification visits (farms 90+ days old: 2 visits required)
- Auditor reviews documents, interviews staff, observes practices
- Scores on compliance checklist

**Step 5: Certification Decision**
- Pass: Certificate issued (valid 3 years, use GAP logo on marketing)
- Corrective action: Address gaps, re-audit required
- Fail: Develop plan, retry after improvements

### 9.4 Cost & Timeline

| Item | Cost | Timeline |
|------|------|----------|
| SC-430 Vendor Form | Free | 1 day |
| SC-237A Audit Request | Free | Submit with forms |
| USDA GAP Audit Fee | $500-1,200 | Apply when ready |
| Follow-up/Re-audit | $200-400 | If corrective action needed |
| **Total (First Certification)** | **$700-1,600** | **3-4 months** |

### 12.1 Do This First (Don't Skip)

   - Lease is fastest (30 days)

   - Irrigation is NON-NEGOTIABLE in Florida
   - Budget $3,000-5,000 for system

3. **Get farmers market vendor spot** (by Month 3)
   - This is your market entry point
   - Happens Saturday every week (reliable)
   - Build customer base before expanding

4. **Talk to existing farmers** (Month 1)
   - Learn what works locally
   - Get pest/disease intel for your county
   - Find mentors/peers for long-term support

### 12.2 What Will Likely Kill Your Farm (Avoid These)

- **No water access** - Florida is humid but irrigation is critical for consistency
- **Wrong crop for your soil** - Test soil first, don't guess
- **Undercapitalized** - $45k+ is minimum, not optional
- **No market before planting** - Know where you're selling FIRST
- **Overextended acreage** - Start small (15-20 acres), scale up
- **Underestimating labor** - Farming is physically demanding; budget time/money
- **Ignoring disease/pests** - Alachua humidity = fungal issues; plan now
- **Isolation** - Join farmer networks early, don't go alone
